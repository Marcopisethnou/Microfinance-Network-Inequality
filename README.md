What troubles me about this was that the dataset used in the paper aggregates individuals into a single unit of household.

The author explicitly mentions, "Moreover, we use households as opposed to individuals as nodes. This is because of the nature of the dataset we use. It consists
of a census of households. But, only individuals from 50% of the households were given a detailed survey that included questions on
social network connections. Because of this survey methodology, we do not have data on all of the individuals from the households that were not surveyed. The measures might differ if we were to look at the individual level, however. This is for two reasons. First,
different individuals in the same household may have different levels of wealth. A younger individual may have less accumulated
wealth compared to an older individual, for example. Second, the network itself might be different. People in the same household
may have different social connections. If more data were available our measure can readily be applied to situations where the nodes
are individuals as opposed to households. Our measure is agnostic to the type of node" on page 12-13 about this too.

Because of this, this makes me curious to how the measure of the author's experienced inequality index would change if individuals are not aggregrated into households. I found this paper 
"The illusion of households as entities in social networks," which answers some of my questions. First, it answers to what conditions we can aggregate household data. [will write more about this]
